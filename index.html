Understand Your Mental Wellbeing

Tap any card to learn more. This content is educational and non-diagnostic.

😰

Stress

+

Stress is a natural response to challenges. Short-term stress can be motivating, but chronic stress can affect sleep, focus, and overall health. Learning to manage stress through breaks, exercise, and support can help.

😟

Anxiety

+

Anxiety involves persistent worry that may interfere with daily activities. It's common among students facing academic or social pressures. Techniques like deep breathing and mindfulness can provide relief.

🔥

Burnout

+

Burnout is emotional, physical, and mental exhaustion from prolonged stress. Signs include feeling detached, reduced performance, and cynicism. Rest, boundaries, and seeking help are key to recovery.

😴

Sleep

+

Quality sleep is essential for memory, focus, and emotional regulation. Most young adults need 7–9 hours. Consistent schedules and reducing screen time before bed can improve sleep quality significantly.

🎯

Focus

+

Difficulty focusing can stem from digital distractions, lack of sleep, or high stress. Techniques like the Pomodoro method, limiting notifications, and single-tasking can help improve concentration.

📱

Digital Overload

+

Constant connectivity can lead to information fatigue and reduced attention span. Setting screen time limits, turning off non-essential notifications, and scheduling device-free time can help restore balance.

💫

Emotional Exhaustion

+

Emotional exhaustion occurs when you feel drained from sustained emotional demands. It's common during exam periods or social conflicts. Self-care routines and talking to someone you trust can help.

📚

Academic Pressure

+

The drive to achieve academically can become overwhelming. Setting realistic goals, breaking tasks into smaller steps, and remembering that grades don't define your worth are important perspectives.
